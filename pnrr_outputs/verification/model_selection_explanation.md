**What was completed.** From 298,338 source project rows, cleaning retained 166,521 unique titles across 8 themes. The experiment used 24,000 training titles, 8,000 validation titles and 21,219 test titles. Related programme groups stayed together. Training/validation were sampled to keep CPU work feasible; the full held-out title set was evaluated. Excluded themes: **Giustizia, Pubblica amministrazione**. Exclusion reflects insufficient independent support, not lesser public importance. Removing duplicates reduces template memorization but changes the population to unique titles. Financial allocation, recorded payments, territorial fairness and independently reviewed purposes were not evaluated by this model.

**How well it worked.** Validation selected **MLP**; its held-out accuracy was **44.4%** and its equal-theme score was **0.389**. Logistic regression achieved 42.1% accuracy and 0.381 macro-F1. The proposed 0.80 equal-theme target was **not met**, and the selected neural model's +0.03 improvement goal was **not met** (observed difference +0.008). These goals were chosen for the project, not independently established deployment thresholds.

**Why the 0.80 target is demanding.** It was an aspirational proposal goal, not a literature-backed benchmark shown to be attainable from these titles. Macro-F1 gives each theme equal weight: the two weakest MLP themes were Digitalizzazione (0.008) and Infrastrutture (0.086). If those two scores stayed fixed, even perfect F1 on every other theme would give only 0.762 overall. This is an arithmetic illustration, not an estimated performance ceiling for future models. Short titles omit context, and inherited programme labels can differ from the activity described; these are plausible constraints, not measured label-error rates or proof that 0.80 is impossible. Group isolation deliberately tests unfamiliar programmes rather than repeated templates. By the final monitored epoch, MLP training macro-F1 was 0.983, versus 0.427 on validation; test evaluation uses the best validation checkpoint. This observed gap supports overfitting/generalization difficulty, so simply adding epochs is not a demonstrated remedy. The bounded training sample and narrow tuning are additional constraints whose individual effects were not isolated.

**What 0.389 does and does not mean.** It is not the percentage of correct titles. It shows some transferable category recognition, particularly Scuola, università e ricerca (F1 0.815), under the programme-held-out test. This is useful exploratory evidence for a neural-network learning project, but modest overall predictive quality rather than a strong deployment result. The training-majority reference collapses under the changed class mix, so beating it cannot establish useful quality. The meaningful text baseline already achieves 0.381; there is no task-independent threshold that makes this result good, and weak-theme failures remain consequential.

**Why retain MLP, and why not declare it better than logistic regression?** The selection rule was validation macro-F1, before examining test outcomes. The selected neural candidate scored 0.441 on validation, versus 0.382 for the tuned logistic reference. The assignment requires a neural network, so carrying this candidate through SHAP and graph-export demonstrations is justified as a research choice. On test data its advantage shrank to +0.0084. MLP improved on logistic regression in 5 themes and worsened in 3; the average hides different error costs. Selection therefore does not establish a deployment preference. Logistic regression has fewer moving parts and readable coefficients; its measured tuning cost and test log-loss are reported below. Keep it as the practical reference candidate until replicated neural gains, acceptable theme-level errors and a validated review policy justify additional complexity. Neither model is approved for automated public decisions.

**What uncertainty allows us to say.** MLP minus logistic regression was +0.0084 macro-F1. Its exploratory 95% interval was [-0.0323, +0.0422], which does not support a reliable positive difference. The one-sided 95% lower bound was -0.0249, not above zero. CNN minus MLP was -0.1011, with interval [-0.1777, +0.0012]. Only 24 independent test groups support these intervals; missing categories in resamples and one split/seed limit certainty. The original hypothesis about independently reviewed purposes remains untested; an inconclusive interval does not prove that models are equivalent.

**Where it struggles.** MLP's strongest theme was **Scuola, università e ricerca** (F1 0.815, 5,714 test titles) and weakest was **Digitalizzazione** (F1 0.008, 278 titles). A successful large theme cannot justify automatic treatment of every theme. The confusion matrices show where recorded themes are redirected; confident disagreement examples suggest possible reliance on institutional wording or ambiguity between overlapping activities. Those cases need targeted explanation and domain review. Some disagreements may expose ambiguous programme labels rather than an incorrect description of actual activity.

**Why the simplest reference can look unusually poor.** The training-majority theme was **Digitalizzazione**, but it represented only 1.3% of held-out titles. Always choosing it therefore gave 1.3% test accuracy. This reflects the changed mix of programmes under group isolation; it is not evidence that every simple classifier is unhelpful. The tuned logistic model is the more informative language benchmark. Unknown CNN words rose from 3.4% in training to 15.7% in test titles. This is a warning of changed language, not proof of a single cause of model failure.

**Scores need caution.** MLP's probability-error measure was 1.652, versus 1.488 for logistic regression (lower is better), so MLP was worse on this measure. Better theme assignments can coexist with poorer scores on confident errors. A displayed 90% model score is not evidence that 90 of 100 similar suggestions will be correct. Calibration and an independently validated review/abstention policy are needed before operational use.

Combined dropout/weight decay changed MLP validation macro-F1 from 0.425 to 0.441, and CNN from 0.395 to 0.382; the selected variants were mlp regularized and cnn plain. This does not isolate dropout from weight decay or establish an optimal setting. mlp plain, mlp regularized, cnn plain, cnn regularized fit their training titles better while their validation loss rose. This warns that continued fitting can strengthen patterns that fail on unfamiliar programmes. CNN's two fits took 11.21 minutes versus 9.26 seconds for the two MLP fits (about 72.6 times as much timed work). All neural fits/monitoring took 11.37 minutes; logistic tuning took 2.32 seconds. These timings depend on hardware and do not include every notebook operation. The energy table assumes power levels; electricity and emissions were not measured. Added complexity should be justified by quality, not by model size alone.

**What explanations establish.** The 2 selected word explanations help a reviewer challenge a suggestion by seeing which title words raised or lowered its score. The largest second-seed attribution change was 4.64 percentage points; at least 3/3 leading word positions overlapped across seeds. The largest reconstruction error was 4.5e-08. These narrow checks concern calculation consistency and sampling variation, not causal truth or fairness. Deleting words and using an empty-title reference affect the answer; explanations can also create false reassurance.
