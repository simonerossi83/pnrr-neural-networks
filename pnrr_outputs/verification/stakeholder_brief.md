**Purpose and coverage.** This project tests whether short Italian project titles can help reviewers organize PNRR records. It retained 166,521 unique titles from 298,338 project rows, and tested 21,219 titles from 24 separate programme groups. Repeated titles were collapsed to avoid learning the same template repeatedly. Themes excluded for insufficient independent examples: **Giustizia, Pubblica amministrazione**. The result covers these titles and themes, not all public investments.

**Main finding.** The validation comparison selected **MLP**. It matched the recorded theme on **44.4%** of test titles, versus **42.1%** for the simpler logistic model. Its balanced, equal-theme score was **0.389**, versus 0.381. The project goals of a 0.80 balanced score and a +0.03 neural improvement were **not met** and **not met**, respectively. These are project goals, not approved standards for deciding funding or eligibility.

**Why the goal was ambitious.** The proposed 0.80 score was an aspiration, not an established standard for this dataset. Short titles can omit a project's purpose, and the recorded programme theme can describe something broader than its activity. Testing separate programmes also exposes unfamiliar wording. The learning curves show much stronger results on training titles than on new programmes. These observations and plausible constraints explain the difficulty; they do not prove that 0.80 is impossible. Better labels and independent evaluation should come before assuming more training will solve it.

**Is 0.389 good?** It is useful early evidence that titles contain category clues, especially for Scuola, università e ricerca, but modest overall performance. It is not the share of correct titles and does not indicate dependable service quality. A successful learning project can still produce a model that needs substantial improvement.

**Why the neural model remains in the project.** Before the final test, MLP's score on the separate validation examples was 0.441, versus 0.382 for logistic regression. That supports selecting it for this neural-network experiment and its explanation/export demonstrations. It does not show that the small final-test difference warrants deployment. The simpler logistic model remains a practical reference candidate; any extra neural complexity should earn a repeatable benefit. Neither model is ready to make public decisions independently.

**What remains uncertain.** MLP's balanced-score difference from logistic regression was +0.008; the estimated uncertainty range was [-0.032, +0.042]. The uncertainty range crosses zero, so a reliable advantage has not been established. Only one programme split and one starting seed were tested. Recorded programme themes are provisional labels: a school-related project may belong to an inclusion programme. No independent reviewers checked the actual purposes, so the original research hypothesis remains untested.

**Who needs particular attention.** The strongest theme was **Scuola, università e ricerca**, with balanced class score 0.815; the weakest was **Digitalizzazione**, with 0.008. Performance on one theme cannot justify automation across all themes. Confident disagreements involving universities suggest possible reliance on institutional wording; these cases need targeted explanation and review. The most common training theme, Digitalizzazione, was only 1.3% of test titles; this changed mix explains the unusually weak always-pick-one-theme reference. The simpler text classifier is the more useful comparison.

**Cost and transparency.** The CNN's two training comparisons took 11.21 minutes, versus 9.26 seconds for the MLP comparisons, and produced a lower balanced score. More computation should earn a practical quality benefit. Electricity and emissions were not measured. The 2 word explanations help reviewers question suggestions; a convincing explanation cannot establish correctness. Implementation checks passed, including checks that saving/reloading preserves sampled predictions. Working software is not evidence of safe independent decision-making, and a high model score is not a verified chance of being right.

**Responsibilities before a pilot.** These are proposed next steps, not completed approvals.

| Owner | Action | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Data steward and domain reviewers | Check a sample across themes, territories and organizations; keep unknowns and corrections visible. | Inherited labels and gaps in coverage can misrepresent activities. |
| Analyst and named deployment owner | Validate a new snapshot and a human-review rule; monitor errors and suspend unsupported uses. | Unfamiliar wording and weak themes can cause confident mistakes. |
| Publisher and service owner | Give citizens and project organizations readable source records and a correction/appeal channel. | Incorrect categories can hide work or distort public perceptions. |
| Publisher and governance/privacy lead | Review names/addresses, minimize title logging, verify attribution/licence, and document accountability. | Public source data can still expose people; cleaning is not anonymization. |

Proceed only to a supervised category-routing pilot after independent label review and agreed review/stop rules. Keep a human responsible for each decision and preserve source categories. Never use predictions alone to award or deny funding, rank communities or allege misconduct. Review adds work, but protects affected communities while allowing administrators to explore useful assistance.

**Reading the map and deciding what comes next.** `italy_projects.html` describes recorded projects and programme themes, not these predictions; it is not regenerated here. Counts are not funding amounts or payments, and geographic patterns do not establish fairness, misconduct or policy impact. The map needs its own coverage/attribution checks. The notebook and self-contained HTML may accompany each other, subject to the submission platform's HTML policy. Prioritize trustworthy labels, weak-theme review and accountable oversight before a larger model.
